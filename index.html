Contracts versus ordinary wallets

Of the 20 largest holders, 3 are contracts and 16 are ordinary wallets (addresses with no bytecode at the snapshot block). The remaining one is the burn address 0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD, which also has no bytecode but is not an ordinary wallet: tokens there cannot be spent by any known key.

The three contracts are the two PancakeSwap v3 pools (ranks 1 and 2) and the address BscScan labels “Pancakeswap: Infinity Vault” (rank 7). Contract status was decided by reading eth_getCode for each of the 20 addresses at block 129,454,096, not at head.

How the numbers were produced

1. The block

A binary search over block timestamps via eth_getBlockByNumber gives the last block strictly before 2026-10-24 05:00:00 UTC: block 129,454,096 (timestamp 2026-10-24 04:59:59 UTC, i.e. 2026-10-23 23:59:59 COT); block 129,454,097 carries 2026-10-24 05:00:00 UTC. No averaging, no “nearest day”.

2. Total supply and each balance

At exactly that block: totalSupply() returns 44444444444000000000000000000, which is 44,444,444,444.000000 FLAPJAX with the token’s 18 decimals. Each holder balance is the return value of balanceOf(address) at the same block.

3. Why this is the top 20 and not a guess at it

The candidate addresses come from BscScan’s public holder table (a chain index, used only to enumerate candidates — every number in the table is re-read from a node). That enumeration alone cannot prove nobody was missed, so it is closed as follows:

A holder that was in the snapshot top 20 and is not in today’s top 25 must have moved tokens after the snapshot. So every transfer of FLAPJAX from block 129,454,097 to head was pulled with eth_getLogs (47 transfer events, 33 distinct addresses) and the snapshot balance of every one of those addresses was read as well.

The largest snapshot balance found among addresses outside today’s top 25 is 8,220,000.000000 FLAPJAX (0.0185% of supply). Every other address outside the current top 25 that did not move since the snapshot still holds exactly what it held then, which is at most today’s rank-25 balance of 253,650,856.719947 FLAPJAX. The 20th largest holder at the snapshot holds 383,287,277.524235 FLAPJAX (0.8624%). Both bounds sit far below that, so no address outside this table can belong in the top 20 at block 129,454,096.

5. Labels: what is a public record and what is not

Six addresses carry an identification; fourteen are left unlabelled. Each identification names its source in the table above. Two labels (the PancakeSwap pools) were confirmed from contract state, not only from a label; one (the treasury) rests on FlapJax’s own public statement naming that address; two (the deployer and the Infinity Vault) rest on BscScan’s public address labels; and the burn address rests on BscScan’s label plus the fact that it holds no code.

Explicitly not claimed: that any unlabelled address is an exchange, a team wallet or a market maker. There is no public record tying those fourteen addresses to a name, so they stay unlabelled. Saying unknown beats a guess.

Limits of this audit

  • It is a single-block snapshot, not a history. Balances change; the block is named so the numbers can be reproduced exactly.
  • “Contract” means “had bytecode at that block”. It does not distinguish a pool, a vault, a multisig or a bridge.
  • Shares are of totalSupply (44,444,444,444.000000 FLAPJAX), not of circulating supply, which is not defined on-chain.
  • The top-20 boundary is proven exactly for block 129,454,096 only. After the snapshot, holdings changed (for example the treasury address at rank 6 and the Infinity Vault at rank 7 both moved tokens), so this table must not be read as today’s distribution.
